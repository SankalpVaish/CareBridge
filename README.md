## 1. Overview

This document lists the problems we need to understand before we design anything. It contains no solutions. Each question:

- has a unique ID, so the team can refer to it in discussion and in later documents;
- belongs to one workstream, which has a single owner;
- is linked to the observations it investigates (Section 7).

## 2. Problem Statement

A rural patient who needs a specialist faces a chain of gaps. Any one of them can delay or stop care:

1. **Recognition.** The patient may not know how serious a symptom is, or what kind of doctor to see.
2. **Discovery.** The patient doesn't know which providers exist nearby, or which ones to trust.
3. **Availability.** The treatment needed may not be offered locally.
4. **Affordability.** Costs are unclear, budgets are limited, and financial aid is hard to find.
5. **Coordination.** Once a referral is made, scheduling, travel, and communication between care teams often break down.
6. **Continuity.** Conflicting prescriptions for the same condition erode trust, so patients switch doctors and restart care.

The result is delayed diagnosis and treatment, wasted trips and money, exposure to fraud, and patients giving up on care altogether.

## 3. Scope

**In scope**
- Rural access to specialty care
- Referral coordination
- Appointment scheduling
- Transportation
- Telehealth
- Insurance coverage constraints
- Administrative burden
- Communication between patients, specialists, and primary-care teams

**Out of scope**
- **Clinical decisions.** Diagnosis, treatment choice, and prescribing remain with qualified professionals.
- **Solution design.** Covered in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).

## 4. Observations

Each observation states who is affected, what the problem is, and what it leads to.

| ID | Observation | Workstream |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Patients can't tell how serious a symptom is or what kind of care it needs, so they delay care, self-medicate, or see the wrong provider first. | A |
| O2 | Patients know only the providers their family and neighbors mention, not everyone within reach. | B |
| O3 | Hospitals specialize (e.g. neurology, cancer), but what each one offers isn't visible to patients or to the clinics referring them. | B |
| O4 | Patients have no reliable way to choose between doctors, even between doctors at the same hospital who treat the same condition. | B |
| O5 | Patients often don't know whether a specialist is available, or how long the wait is, until they arrive. | B, C |
| O6 | Many specialist services don't exist locally, so patients must travel to distant centers they know nothing about. | B |
| O7 | Unlicensed practitioners, middlemen (touts), and "guaranteed cure" clinics take advantage of patients who don't know where to go, and patients can't check who is legitimate. | B |
| O8 | The full cost of care, including travel, lodging, and lost wages, isn't known up front and often exceeds what families can pay. | A |
| O9 | Insurance, government schemes, and financial aid exist, but patients can't easily find out what they qualify for or how to apply. | A |
| O10 | Different doctors prescribe different medicines for the same condition, so patients lose trust and keep switching doctors. | C |
| O11 | Referrals often stall: specialists receive incomplete information, and PCPs rarely learn whether the patient was seen. | C |
| O12 | Seeing a specialist can take several trips and a full day of travel, and every follow-up repeats it. | C |
| O13 | Remote consultations exist in some places, but rural patients rarely use them. | C |
| O14 | Patients often leave a specialist visit without understanding what was said or what to do next. | C |
| O15 | Paperwork such as referral letters, pre-authorization, and scheme claims takes up clinic time and delays care. | A, C |

## 5. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | How the problem affects them |
|---|---|
| Patients and families | Delayed care, wasted trips, unexpected costs, exposure to fraud |
| Community health workers (CHWs) | Often the first point of contact, but lack reliable information on where to send people |
| Primary-care providers (PCPs) and rural clinics | Refer patients out, then lose visibility of what happened |
| Specialists and hospitals | Receive incomplete referrals, no-shows, and patients who come too late |
| Insurers and government schemes | Coverage rules and paperwork that patients often don't understand |
| NGOs, charities, and scholarship funders | Aid goes unclaimed because eligible people never learn it exists |

### 6.1 Workstream A: Care Need and Affordability

**A.1 Recognizing the need**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| A-1 | How do rural patients decide that a symptom needs a doctor, and what makes them wait? |
| A-2 | Who do they ask first (family, CHW, pharmacist, traditional healer, internet), and how reliable is that advice? |
| A-3 | How do people tell an emergency from something that can wait? What happens when they get it wrong? |
| A-4 | How often do patients go to the wrong kind of doctor first, and what does that cost them in time and money? |
| A-5 | What words do people use to describe their symptoms, and how far is that from medical language? |
| A-6 | Where is the line between general health guidance and a clinical judgment that only a professional should make? |

**A.2 Cost, coverage, and financial aid**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| A-7 | What does specialist care cost a rural family from start to finish (consultation, tests, treatment, medicines, travel, lodging, lost wages)? |
| A-8 | How much of that cost is known before care starts? How often are families surprised by bills? |
| A-9 | Which insurance or government schemes do rural patients hold, and do they know what is covered? |
| A-10 | Which coverage rules block or delay care (approved-hospital lists, pre-authorization, claim paperwork)? |
| A-11 | What scholarships, charity programs, or NGO aid exist for treatment, travel, or lodging, and why do they go unclaimed? |
| A-12 | What documents and proof of eligibility do these programs require? Which are hardest for rural families to produce? |
| A-13 | How do budget limits change patients' decisions (delaying care, picking cheaper but less suitable providers, stopping treatment partway)? |

**A.3 Barriers on the patient's side**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| A-14 | Which languages, literacy levels, and digital skills must be accounted for? |
| A-15 | What phones and internet access do patients have? Are phones shared within a household? |
| A-16 | What role do CHWs and family members play in helping someone get care? |

### 6.2 Workstream B: Provider Discovery and Trust

**B.1 Awareness of providers**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| B-1 | How do people find out today which hospitals, clinics, and specialists exist in their region? |
| B-2 | What public information about providers exists today, and how accurate and up to date is it? |
| B-3 | How far are people willing and able to travel to see a specialist, and what limits that? |

**B.2 Specialization and treatment availability**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| B-4 | How do patients learn which hospitals specialize in which conditions? |
| B-5 | Which specialties and treatments are most often missing in rural areas? |
| B-6 | When a treatment isn't available locally, how do people find the nearest place that offers it? |
| B-7 | How long do people wait for a specialist appointment, and how do they find out who has openings? |

**B.3 Quality and comparison**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| B-8 | What do patients mean by a "good doctor" (communication, results, cost, wait time, language, attitude)? |
| B-9 | What quality signals exist today (word of mouth, online reviews, accreditation), and how trustworthy are they? |
| B-10 | What would a fair comparison of doctors who treat the same condition at the same hospital need to account for, given that patient experience is not the same as clinical outcome? |
| B-11 | How do ratings get distorted (fake reviews, too few reviews, penalties for doctors who take harder cases)? |

**B.4 Trust and fraud**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| B-12 | What kinds of healthcare fraud target rural patients (fake doctors, "guaranteed cure" clinics, middlemen, upfront-payment schemes)? |
| B-13 | Can patients check whether a doctor is licensed or a hospital is accredited? How do they check today? |
| B-14 | Which official registries and accreditation bodies exist, and how easy is it to get their data? |
| B-15 | What makes a scam convincing, and which warning signs do victims miss? |

### 6.3 Workstream C: Care Delivery and Coordination

**C.1 Referral coordination**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-1 | Step by step, how does a referral from a PCP to a specialist work today, and where does it break down? |
| C-2 | What share of referrals never lead to a specialist visit, and why? |
| C-3 | What information does a specialist need from the PCP, and what is usually missing when the referral arrives? |
| C-4 | When a referral stalls, who is responsible for following up: the patient, the PCP, or the specialist? |

**C.2 Appointment scheduling**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-5 | How do patients book specialist appointments today (phone, walk-in, agent, queue)? How many attempts or trips does it take? |
| C-6 | How common are no-shows and wasted trips (e.g. the doctor is unavailable on arrival), and what causes them? |
| C-7 | When an appointment changes, how do patients find out? |

**C.3 Transportation**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-8 | How do rural patients get to specialist care, and what does the trip cost in money and time? |
| C-9 | Which transport options exist (public bus, shared rides, community transport, government ambulance or non-emergency transport), and do people know about them? |
| C-10 | How often is travel the main reason care is delayed or abandoned? |
| C-11 | What extra burdens come with the trip (a companion, lodging, lost wages, childcare)? |

**C.4 Telehealth**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-12 | Which parts of specialty care can realistically happen remotely (first consultation, follow-ups, reviewing test results), as judged by clinicians? |
| C-13 | What stops rural patients from using telehealth: internet access, devices, trust, language, cost, or not knowing it exists? |
| C-14 | Do specialists and hospitals in the region offer telehealth, and do insurance and schemes cover it? |

**C.5 Communication between patient, PCP, and specialist**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-15 | After a specialist visit, does the PCP find out what happened? How? |
| C-16 | Do patients understand what the specialist told them? What gets lost (jargon, language, rushed visits)? |
| C-17 | How do patients ask follow-up questions once they are back home? |
| C-18 | Who holds the patient's records, and can the patient take them from one provider to another? |

**C.6 Different prescriptions for the same condition**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-19 | How often do patients get different medicines from different doctors for the same condition? |
| C-20 | Why do prescriptions for the same condition differ (clinical judgment, patient history, which medicines are available locally, brand vs. generic, cost, commercial incentives)? Which of these reasons are explained to patients? |
| C-21 | How does this affect patients' trust in their treatment, and how often does it lead them to switch doctors? |
| C-22 | What does switching doctors cost patients (money, time, repeated tests, treatment restarted from scratch)? Does the new doctor know what was prescribed before? |

**C.7 Administrative burden**

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| C-23 | Which paperwork steps take the most time for clinics and patients (referral letters, pre-authorization, scheme claims, registration forms)? |
| C-24 | Which of these steps make people enter the same information again and again? |
| C-25 | Where do administrative delays directly delay care? |

### 6.4 Cross-Cutting Questions

| ID | Theme | Question |
|---|---|---|
| X-1 | Clinical boundaries | Which decisions must always stay with a qualified clinician? |
| X-2 | Clinical boundaries | Which emergency warning signs must never be delayed by any administrative or navigation step? |
| X-3 | Privacy and consent | What health information are patients willing to share, with whom, and with what kind of consent? |
| X-4 | Privacy and consent | Which regulations apply in the target region (e.g. HIPAA in the US; DPDP Act 2023 and ABDM in India)? |
| X-5 | Equity | Who is most likely to be left out: older adults, women, people with low literacy, people without phones, people with disabilities, speakers of minority languages? |
| X-6 | Equity | How might existing quality signals, such as ratings and reputation, unfairly disadvantage rural or government providers? |
| X-7 | Problem sizing | How big is each problem today? Candidate baselines: days from first symptom to specialist visit, referral completion rate, trips per completed visit, out-of-pocket cost compared with household budget. |
| X-8 | Problem sizing | Which problem is the biggest bottleneck, the one whose resolution would matter most? |
| X-9 | Context | Which region is the focus? The answer decides which schemes, registries, languages, and currency apply. |
