## 1. Overview

This document lists the problems we need to understand before we design anything. It contains no solutions. Each question:

- has a unique ID, so the team can refer to it in discussion and in later documents;
- belongs to one workstream, which has a single owner;
- is linked to at least one observation from the field (Section 7).

## 2. Problem Statement

A rural patient who needs a specialist faces a chain of gaps. Any one of them can delay or stop care:

1. **Recognition.** The patient may not know what is wrong, or what kind of doctor to see.
2. **Discovery.** The patient doesn't know which providers exist nearby, or which ones to trust.
3. **Availability.** The treatment needed may not be offered locally.
4. **Affordability.** Costs are unclear, budgets are limited, and financial aid is hard to find.
5. **Coordination.** Once a referral is made, scheduling, travel, and communication between care teams often break down.

The result is delayed diagnosis and treatment, wasted trips and money, exposure to fraud, and patients giving up on care altogether.

## 3. Scope

**In scope**
- Rural access to specialty care
- Referral coordination
- Appointment scheduling
- Transportation
- Telehealth
- Insurance coverage constraints
- Administrative burden
- Communication between patients, specialists, and primary-care teams

**Out of scope**
- **Clinical decisions.** Diagnosis, treatment choice, and prescribing remain with qualified professionals.
- **Solution design.** Covered in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).

## 5. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | How the problem affects them |
|---|---|
| Patients and families | Delayed care, wasted trips, unexpected costs, exposure to fraud |
| Community health workers (CHWs) | Often the first point of contact, but lack reliable information on where to send people |
| Primary-care providers (PCPs) and rural clinics | Refer patients out, then lose visibility of what happened |
| Specialists and hospitals | Receive incomplete referrals, no-shows, and patients who come too late |
| Insurers and government schemes | Coverage rules and paperwork that patients often don't understand |
| NGOs, charities, and scholarship funders | Aid goes unclaimed because eligible people never learn it exists |

### 6.1 Workstream A: Care Need and Affordability

| ID | Question | Status |

## 8. Research Method and Evidence Log

### 8.1 Sources

- **Interviews:** patients and caregivers, CHWs, PCPs and clinic staff, specialists, hospital administrators
- **Public data:** health facility registries, medical council and licensing registries, government scheme documentation, census and travel-distance data
- **Literature:** peer-reviewed studies and government or NGO reports on rural access, referral completion, and out-of-pocket spending
- **Desk review:** existing directories, review sites, and reported fraud cases

### 8.2 Status and confidence definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Open | Not yet investigated |
| In progress | Evidence is being gathered |
| Answered | Supported by at least one cited source |

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Single anecdote or unverified source |
| Medium | Several consistent sources, or one reliable dataset |
| High | Several independent, reliable sources that agree |
