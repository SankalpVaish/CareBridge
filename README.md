# RQ1: Rural Specialty-Care Access: Questions We Need to Answer

> **Research question (RQ1):** What stops rural patients from getting timely specialty care? Areas in scope: rural specialty-care access, referral coordination, appointment scheduling, transportation, telehealth, insurance coverage constraints, administrative burden, and communication between patients, specialists, and primary-care teams.
>
> **Boundary:** Clinical decisions remain with qualified professionals. Our job is to understand the barriers that keep patients from reaching them.

This README covers **problems and open questions only**. Proposed solutions live in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).

---

## The problem in brief

A rural patient who needs a specialist runs into a chain of gaps:
- They may not know what is wrong or what kind of doctor to see.
- They don't know which providers exist nearby, or which ones to trust.
- The treatment they need may not be offered locally.
- Costs are unclear and budgets are tight.
- Once a referral is made, scheduling, travel, and communication between doctors often break down.

Each gap delays care. Some push people toward scammers or toward giving up on treatment altogether.

## What we have observed

1. People often don't know what condition they might have.
2. People don't know about the many healthcare providers in their area.
3. Hospitals specialize in different conditions (e.g. neurology hospitals, cancer hospitals). It isn't clear which one fits a patient's need.
4. It's hard to find out a doctor's availability and quality, so people can't tell which doctor would serve them best.
5. Within one hospital, several doctors may treat the same condition, and patients have no way to compare them.
6. Different doctors prescribe different medicines to the same patient, and nobody sees the full picture.
7. Many treatments aren't available in rural areas. People don't know which reputable hospitals are within reach, and scammers exploit that gap.
8. Costs vary widely, budgets are limited, and the scholarships and financial aid that exist are hard to find.

## Who is affected

| Group | How the problem reaches them |
|---|---|
| Patients and families | Delayed care, wasted trips, surprise costs, exposure to scams |
| Community health workers (CHWs) | Often the first contact, but lack information on where to send people |
| Primary-care providers (PCPs) and rural clinics | Refer patients out, then lose track of what happened |
| Specialists and hospitals | Receive incomplete referrals, no-shows, and patients who arrive too late |
| Insurers and government schemes | Coverage rules and paperwork that patients don't understand |
| NGOs, charities, and scholarship funders | Aid goes unclaimed because eligible people never hear about it |

## Questions we need to answer

Each section has one owner, matching the team split in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md). Everyone shares the cross-cutting questions.

### Section A: Understanding the need and what it will cost (Person 1)

**Recognizing the need**
- [ ] **A-1** How do rural patients decide a symptom needs a doctor, and what makes them wait?
- [ ] **A-2** Who do they ask first (family, CHW, pharmacist, traditional healer, internet), and how reliable is that advice?
- [ ] **A-3** How do people tell an emergency from something that can wait? What happens when they get it wrong?
- [ ] **A-4** How often do patients go to the wrong kind of doctor first, and what does that cost them in time and money?
- [ ] **A-5** What words do people use to describe their symptoms, and how far is that from medical language?
- [ ] **A-6** Where is the line between general health guidance and a clinical judgment that only a professional should make?

**Cost, coverage, and financial aid**
- [ ] **A-7** What does specialist care actually cost a rural family from start to finish (consultation, tests, treatment, medicines, travel, lodging, lost wages)?
- [ ] **A-8** How much of that cost is known before care starts? How often are families surprised by bills?
- [ ] **A-9** Which insurance or government schemes do rural patients hold, and do they know what is covered?
- [ ] **A-10** Which coverage rules block or delay care (approved-hospital lists, pre-authorization, claim paperwork)?
- [ ] **A-11** What scholarships, charity programs, or NGO aid exist for treatment, travel, or lodging, and why do they go unclaimed?
- [ ] **A-12** What documents and proof of eligibility do these programs require? Which ones are hardest for rural families to produce?
- [ ] **A-13** How do budget limits change patients' decisions (delaying care, picking cheaper but less suitable providers, stopping treatment partway)?

**Barriers on the patient's side**
- [ ] **A-14** Which languages, literacy levels, and digital skills do we need to account for?
- [ ] **A-15** What phones and internet access do patients have? Are phones shared within a household?
- [ ] **A-16** What role do CHWs and family members play in helping someone get care?

### Section B: Finding providers and knowing whom to trust (Person 2)

**Awareness of providers**
- [ ] **B-1** How do people find out today which hospitals, clinics, and specialists exist in their region?
- [ ] **B-2** What public information about providers exists today, and how accurate and up to date is it?
- [ ] **B-3** How far are people willing and able to travel to see a specialist, and what limits that?

**Specialization and treatment availability**
- [ ] **B-4** How do patients learn which hospitals specialize in which conditions?
- [ ] **B-5** Which specialties and treatments are most often missing in rural areas?
- [ ] **B-6** When a treatment isn't available locally, how do people find the nearest place that offers it?
- [ ] **B-7** How long do people wait for a specialist appointment, and how do they find out who has openings?

**Quality and comparison**
- [ ] **B-8** What do patients mean by a "good doctor"? (Communication, results, cost, wait time, language, attitude?)
- [ ] **B-9** What quality signals exist today (word of mouth, online reviews, accreditation), and how trustworthy are they?
- [ ] **B-10** What would a fair comparison of doctors who treat the same condition at the same hospital need to account for? Patient experience is not the same as clinical outcome.
- [ ] **B-11** How do ratings get distorted (fake reviews, too few reviews, penalties for doctors who take harder cases)?

**Trust and scams**
- [ ] **B-12** What kinds of healthcare scams target rural patients (fake doctors, "guaranteed cure" clinics, middlemen, upfront-payment fraud)?
- [ ] **B-13** Can patients check whether a doctor is licensed or a hospital is accredited? How do they check today?
- [ ] **B-14** Which official registries and accreditation bodies exist, and how easy is it to get their data?
- [ ] **B-15** What makes a scam convincing, and which warning signs do victims miss?

### Section C: Getting care and keeping everyone connected (Person 3)

**Referral coordination**
- [ ] **C-1** Step by step, how does a referral from a PCP to a specialist work today, and where does it break down?
- [ ] **C-2** What share of referrals never lead to a specialist visit, and why?
- [ ] **C-3** What information does a specialist need from the PCP, and what is usually missing when the referral arrives?
- [ ] **C-4** When a referral stalls, who is responsible for following up: the patient, the PCP, or the specialist?

**Appointment scheduling**
- [ ] **C-5** How do patients book specialist appointments today (phone, walk-in, agent, queue)? How many attempts or trips does it take?
- [ ] **C-6** How common are no-shows and wasted trips (e.g. the patient arrives and the doctor is unavailable), and what causes them?
- [ ] **C-7** When an appointment changes, how do patients find out?

**Transportation**
- [ ] **C-8** How do rural patients get to specialist care, and what does the trip cost in money and time?
- [ ] **C-9** Which transport options exist (public bus, shared rides, community transport, government ambulance or non-emergency transport), and do people know about them?
- [ ] **C-10** How often is travel the main reason care is delayed or abandoned?
- [ ] **C-11** What extra burdens come with the trip (a companion, lodging, lost wages, childcare)?

**Telehealth**
- [ ] **C-12** Which parts of specialty care can realistically happen remotely (first consultation, follow-ups, reviewing test results), as judged by clinicians?
- [ ] **C-13** What stops rural patients from using telehealth: internet access, devices, trust, language, cost, or not knowing it exists?
- [ ] **C-14** Do specialists and hospitals in the region offer telehealth, and do insurance and schemes cover it?

**Communication between patient, PCP, and specialist**
- [ ] **C-15** After a specialist visit, does the PCP find out what happened? How?
- [ ] **C-16** Do patients understand what the specialist told them? What gets lost (jargon, language, rushed visits)?
- [ ] **C-17** How do patients ask follow-up questions once they are back home?
- [ ] **C-18** Who holds the patient's records, and can the patient take them from one provider to another?

**Medicines from multiple doctors**
- [ ] **C-19** How often do patients see several doctors who each prescribe medicines without knowing what the others prescribed?
- [ ] **C-20** What risks does this create (duplicate medicines, interactions, confusion about what to take)? Who is in a position to catch them?
- [ ] **C-21** Why do prescriptions differ between doctors (clinical judgment, local availability, brand vs. generic, cost)? How much of this does the patient understand?
- [ ] **C-22** How do patients keep track of what they are currently taking?

**Administrative burden**
- [ ] **C-23** Which paperwork steps take the most time for clinics and patients (referral letters, pre-authorization, scheme claims, registration forms)?
- [ ] **C-24** Which of these steps make people enter the same information again and again?
- [ ] **C-25** Where do administrative delays directly delay care?

### Cross-cutting questions (everyone)

**Safety and clinical boundaries**
- [ ] **X-1** Which decisions must always stay with a qualified clinician?
- [ ] **X-2** Which emergency warning signs must never be delayed by any navigation or paperwork step?

**Privacy and consent**
- [ ] **X-3** What health information are patients willing to share, with whom, and with what kind of consent?
- [ ] **X-4** Which regulations apply in our target region (e.g. HIPAA in the US; DPDP Act 2023 and ABDM in India)?

**Equity**
- [ ] **X-5** Who is most likely to be left out: older adults, women, people with low literacy, people without phones, people with disabilities, speakers of minority languages?
- [ ] **X-6** How might existing quality signals, such as ratings and reputation, unfairly disadvantage rural or government providers?

**Sizing the problem**
- [ ] **X-7** How big is each problem today? Possible baselines: days from first symptom to specialist visit, referral completion rate, trips per completed visit, out-of-pocket cost compared with household budget, share of patients who can name a verified specialist nearby.
- [ ] **X-8** Which problem is the biggest bottleneck? If only one could be fixed, which would matter most?
- [ ] **X-9** Which region are we focusing on? The answer decides which schemes, registries, languages, and currency apply.
